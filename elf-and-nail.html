Scope

Scope of this guide

Year position: The archetype peaks in the Darksteel/Skullclamp environment and is distinct from the slower Tron Tooth deck seen at Worlds and after rotation.
1 · Deck identity

Elf and Nail

Elf and Nail uses small green creatures as mana, cards, artifact interaction, and Tooth and Nail fuel. Skullclamp prevents the normal creature-ramp problem of running out of material, while Vernal Bloom and Wood Elves make single Forests produce extraordinary amounts of mana.

2 · Game plan

What this deck does

Accelerate with Birds, Trellis, and Wood Elves.

Use Wirewood Symbiote to reuse Viridian Shaman or save an Elf while untapping another creature.

Turn small creatures into two cards with Skullclamp.

Resolve Vernal Bloom or Tooth and Nail, then win through Kamahl, Colossus, Triskelion, or Sundering Titan.

3 · Development

History and evolution

Before Darksteel

Tooth is a spell-based big-mana deck

Cloudpost and land search dominate early builds.

Darksteel

Skullclamp creates a creature engine

Wirewood Herald, Symbiote, Wood Elves, and Shaman become cards as well as board presence.

U.S. Nationals

Craig Krempels wins

The deck proves that the strongest Skullclamp shell is not necessarily Affinity.

After June 20

The engine loses Clamp

Traditional Tooth and Nail survives, but the Elf-heavy branch loses its main reason to exist.

4 · Construction

Key design characteristics

High creature density

The ramp, interaction, and draw engine all require creatures; this creates explosive synergy but vulnerability to sweepers.

Forest multiplication

Vernal Bloom rewards basic Forests and Wood Elves, while the red splash exists mainly for Pyroclasm.

Toolbox finish

Tooth packages change by matchup: Colossus, Duplicant, Triskelion, Kamahl, or Sundering Titan.

Artifact-aware maindeck

Viridian Shaman and sideboard Oxidize ensure the deck does not merely race Affinity.

6 · Opening decisions

Mulligans and opening turns

Keep

  • Two lands with Birds/Trellis/Wood Elves.
  • Skullclamp plus expendable creatures.
  • A mana-heavy hand that can realistically cast Vernal Bloom or Tooth by turn four or five.

Send back

  • Hands of expensive Tooth targets without acceleration.
  • One-land hands dependent on Birds surviving.
  • Creature-light hands where Skullclamp cannot draw cards.

Opening priorities

  1. Choose whether Wood Elves should develop mana or be saved for Symbiote loops.
  2. Do not expose every mana creature to Pyroclasm when one Vernal Bloom can supply the required mana.
  3. Plan the Tooth pair before tapping mana; Duplicant or Triskelion may be more important than the biggest attacker.
7 · Rules and sequencing

Key interactions

Wirewood Symbiote returns an Elf as a cost and can untap another creature once each turn.

Skullclamp can deliberately kill one-toughness creatures and draw two cards.

Kamahl can animate lands, then pump the entire team; be careful animating lands into opposing sweepers.

Vernal Bloom affects every Forest, including opposing Forests.

8 · Play patterns

How to pilot the deck

  1. Use the first turns to create a mana and card engine, not to deal incidental combat damage.
  2. Sequence Skullclamp so removal cannot strand it without a creature to equip.
  3. Against control, hold a second mana engine after the first sweeper.
  4. Use Tooth and Nail as a flexible tutor rather than automatically choosing Colossus plus Kamahl.
9 · Post-board roles

Sideboarding principles

Affinity

Oxidize, Pyroclasm, and Viridian Shaman enter; avoid a pure goldfish plan.

Control

Forgotten Ancient and Sundering Titan punish slow games; preserve threats after Wrath.

Creature decks

Pyroclasm and Triskelion can reset the board while Clamp rebuilds.

Mirror / Tooth

Sundering Titan and mana denial matter more than creature combat.
