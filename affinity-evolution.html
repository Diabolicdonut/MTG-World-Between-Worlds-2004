1 · Identity

Affinity Across the Worlds Cycle

Affinity changes more than any other successful deck between Berlin and San Francisco. It does not exist at Worlds 2003; becomes an Atog/Enforcer artifact-land deck after Mirrodin; gains Ravager and Skullclamp from Darksteel; survives the ban by adopting Cranial Plating; and remains powerful enough to place three decks in the Worlds 2004 Top 8.

2 · Development

Four stages

October 2003

Artifact lands and affinity establish the engine

Frogmite, Myr Enforcer, Thoughtcast, Atog, Disciple, and artifact lands create a deck where mana sources also count toward spell discounts and sacrifice damage.

February 2004

Ravager and Skullclamp compress every resource

Darksteel gives the deck a modular sacrifice outlet and the strongest card-draw engine in Standard.

June 20

Skullclamp is banned

The deck loses raw card flow but retains Ravager, Disciple, artifact lands, free creatures, and Thoughtcast.

Worlds 2004

Cranial Plating becomes the new damage engine

Paquette, Cornelissen, and Evans all reach the Top 8 with different configurations.

3 · Design

Why the shell survives every change

Mana is also material

Artifact lands cast spells, reduce affinity costs, increase Plating, and can be sacrificed.

Threat mobility

Ravager modular and Plating move damage away from the permanent an opponent chose to answer.

Multiple kill axes

Combat, Disciple drains, Atog, Nexus, Enforcer, and equipment punish different defenses.

Transformational sideboards

Mana Leak, Oxidize, Pyroclasm, Relic Barrier, and Furnace Dragon let the deck change roles.

5 · Piloting

Core sequencing principles

  1. Count artifact permanents before choosing the order of lands, zero-mana creatures, and Thoughtcast.
  2. Do not sacrifice automatically: future Plating, Enforcer, and affinity math may be worth more than one Ravager counter.
  3. Under period combat rules, assigned damage can remain on the stack while creatures are sacrificed.
  4. Force anti-artifact decks to answer different permanent types and zones rather than one large board.
6 · Cycle matchups

How the enemy changes

OpponentKey pressure
Mirari’s WakeCross-era only: Affinity’s speed punishes a deck built before artifact lands existed.
Astral SlideViridian Shaman, Oxidize, Wrath, and Slide combat control make this the defining anti-Affinity opponent.
Skullclamp GoblinsBoth decks generate cards from expendable creatures; Plating or Disciple usually breaks stalled boards.
Elf and NailRace the mana engine while preventing Viridian Shaman and Pyroclasm from buying multiple turns.
Big RedPyroclasm and red artifact removal punish small boards; Nexus and Enforcer are essential.
U/W ControlAnnul and Wrath matter, but Ravager, Jar, Disciple, and Nexus make sweepers incomplete.
