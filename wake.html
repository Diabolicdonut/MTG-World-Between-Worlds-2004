Scope

Scope of this guide

Worlds-cycle position: This dossier covers the final Odyssey–Onslaught Standard environment at Berlin and the short period immediately afterward, before Mirrodin became legal on October 20, 2003.
1 · Deck identity

Mirari’s Wake Control

Wake was the defining control-combo deck of Worlds 2003. It spent the early game trading cards and preventing damage, then resolved Mirari’s Wake to double its mana and make every Decree, Wish, and flashback spell overwhelming.

2 · Game plan

What this deck does

Develop colors with Krosan Verge and Compulsion while holding Mana Leak.

Use Moment’s Peace and Wrath to turn creature pressure into wasted turns.

Resolve Mirari’s Wake only when it immediately changes the resource balance.

Win with a large Decree, a copied spell through Mirari, or a Wish target.

3 · Development

History and evolution

Odyssey block

Wake becomes a control-combo engine

Mirari’s Wake, Deep Analysis, Moment’s Peace, and Cunning Wish create a deck that can defend cheaply and dominate long games.

Scourge

Decree of Justice supplies an uncounterable-style finisher

Cycling Decree creates Soldiers through a triggered ability and lets the deck win without tapping out.

Worlds 2003

Four Wake decks reach the overall Top 8

Daniel Zink defeats Jin Okamoto in a Wake mirror, while multiple Wake pilots finish the Standard day undefeated.

October 20, 2003

Mirrodin rotation ends the archetype

Odyssey block rotates, taking Wake, Deep Analysis, Compulsion, Moment’s Peace, and the Wish engine with it.

4 · Construction

Key design characteristics

Mostly creatureless game one

Removal-heavy opponents receive few profitable targets; Exalted Angel and Brushhopper can enter after sideboarding.

Wishboard architecture

Cunning Wish turns singleton sideboard instants into maindeck options without sacrificing broad answers.

Mana multiplication

Wake is not merely ramp: it makes every land produce additional mana while also pumping creature finishers.

Layered inevitability

Deep Analysis, Compulsion, flashback, Wish, Decree, Mirari, and Wake all generate advantages on different axes.

6 · Opening decisions

Mulligans and opening turns

Keep

  • Three lands with at least two colors and a turn-two interaction plan.
  • Krosan Verge plus early defense and a draw engine.
  • Hands that can reach Wrath against Goblins or Madness.

Send back

  • Two-land hands whose colors cannot cast Compulsion, Leak, and Wrath on schedule.
  • Hands full of Wake, Mirari, and Decree without early defense.
  • Reactive hands with no card-selection engine in a control mirror.

Opening priorities

  1. Decide whether the first Krosan Verge activation must fix white for Wrath or preserve blue for permission.
  2. Against aggro, use Moment’s Peace to gain a full development turn rather than protecting a few life points.
  3. Against control, resolve Compulsion before committing Wake into open counters.
7 · Rules and sequencing

Key interactions

Mirari’s Wake gives lands an additional mana ability; it does not replace their normal mana production.

Cycling Decree of Justice creates tokens with a triggered ability, so ordinary counterspells cannot counter the token-making effect.

Cunning Wish can obtain only an instant from outside the game; tournament sideboards supply the toolbox.

Mirari copies an instant or sorcery after an additional three mana is paid, allowing enormous late-game exchanges.

8 · Play patterns

How to pilot the deck

Treat life as a resource until a single Wrath or Moment’s Peace erases several opposing turns.

Do not cast Wake simply because you reached five mana; cast it when the opponent cannot convert the tempo window into lethal damage.

Use Compulsion to discard redundant expensive cards and flashback cards while sculpting land drops.

When Wake resolves, plan the entire next turn before tapping: Decree, Wish, flashback, and permission can often all be held together.

9 · Post-board roles

Sideboarding principles

Creature decks

Bring Exalted Angel, Chastise, and specialized fog/removal; trim slow control-mirror cards.

Wake mirror

Stifle, Circular Logic, extra counters, and alternate threats matter more than Wrath.

Goblin Bidding

Ray of Revelation is irrelevant; prioritize graveyard interruption, Wing Shards, and a fast stabilizing plan.

Reanimator

Wish targets and counters must stop the reanimation engine rather than the eventual creature whenever possible.

10 · Berlin gauntlet

Matchup guide

OpponentRolePlan
U/G MadnessControlRespect Circular Logic and Wonder; fog buys time, but Wrath must resolve before Roar tokens and Wurms overwhelm mana.
Goblin BiddingControlWrath is temporary because Bidding rebuilds. Counter or Wish-answer the recursion spell and preserve graveyard pressure.
ReanimatorControlCounter Entomb, Buried Alive, or the reanimation spell according to the hand; Vengeful Dreams answers individual monsters.
Astral SlideInevitabilityWake has stronger raw mana, while Slide has recursive threats. Protect Compulsion and win through Decree rather than creature combat.
Wake mirrorPermissionLand drops, Compulsion, and end-step Decree define the game. Avoid tapping first into the decisive counter exchange.
Post-Mirrodin AffinityHistorical transitionThe archetype rotates before Affinity’s full rise; this matchup belongs only in cross-era gauntlet play.
