Scope: This dossier follows the B/G Cemetery Cloud family from the 2004 Regionals season through its post-Skullclamp Death Cloud descendants before Worlds. It is included because it was a genuine season milestone even though it did not occupy a Worlds 2004 Top 8 seat.
1 · Deck identity

Cemetery Cloud / Death Cloud

Cemetery Cloud was a resource-engine deck disguised as a pile of small green and black creatures. Wirewood Herald, Wood Elves, Viridian Shaman, Rotlung Reanimator, Skullclamp, and Oversold Cemetery made creatures disposable. Death Cloud then compressed hands, lands, life totals, and boards in a way the deck was built to recover from first.

2 · Strategic role

What this deck does

The deck develops mana and card material through creatures, draws aggressively with Skullclamp, and uses its graveyard as a second hand. Once it is ahead in cards or permanent count, Death Cloud removes the opponent’s ability to rebuild. After Skullclamp left Standard, the family became slower and leaned harder on Eternal Witness, Solemn Simulacrum, Ravenous Baloth, and direct artifact hate.

3 · Development

History and evolution

Mirrodin, late 2003

Cemetery shells emerge

Oversold Cemetery and creature-based value engines gain new tools such as Solemn Simulacrum and Viridian Shaman.

Darksteel, February 2004

Death Cloud supplies the payoff

Death Cloud gives B/G decks a scalable reset that attacks every resource at once.

Regionals, April 2004

A real tournament breakthrough

Shiro Nakayama wins a Regionals event with Cemetery Cloud, establishing the deck as more than a theory exercise.

June 20, 2004

Skullclamp is banned

The most explosive draw engine disappears, forcing Death Cloud decks toward Witness, Baloth, Solemn, and more conventional control cards.

Grand Prix Nagoya, August 2004

The post-ban branch remains relevant

Death Cloud is one of the major Day 2 archetypes and reaches the Top 8 in the last major Standard event before Worlds.

4 · Construction logic

Key design characteristics

Disposable permanents

Mana creatures, Heralds, Wood Elves, and utility bodies are useful before being sacrificed or clamped.

Graveyard recursion

Oversold Cemetery and later Eternal Witness turn sacrificed creatures and spells into future resources.

Asymmetrical catastrophe

The deck chooses X and times Death Cloud only after arranging to retain the better post-Cloud board or hand.

Built-in artifact hate

Viridian Shaman, Zealot, and Oxidize let the deck compete in an Affinity-defined environment without filling the deck with dead cards.

6 · Opening decisions

Mulligans and opening turns

Keep

  • A green source, black source, and an accelerator or Wood Elves.
  • Skullclamp plus one-toughness creatures in the pre-ban build.
  • An artifact-interaction hand against known Affinity, even if Death Cloud is not yet present.

Send back

  • Hands with Death Cloud but no acceleration or expendable permanents.
  • One-color hands that cannot cast both the green development cards and black reset spells.
  • Slow Cemetery hands against Affinity with no Shaman, Oxidize, or early body.

Opening priorities

  1. Develop mana and creature count before revealing how large a Death Cloud you intend to cast.
  2. Against Affinity, use Viridian Shaman on the card that slows their entire sequence rather than the first legal artifact.
  3. Against control, sequence Cemetery or Witness so that a countered spell still improves the graveyard engine.
7 · Rules and sequencing

Key interactions

Death Cloud resolves in printed order: each player loses life, discards cards, sacrifices creatures, then sacrifices lands. Plan every category before choosing X.

Oversold Cemetery checks for four creature cards at the beginning of upkeep; creatures sacrificed to a previous Death Cloud can turn it on immediately.

Skullclamp kills one-toughness creatures through the toughness reduction, drawing cards and often triggering Wirewood Herald or Rotlung Reanimator.

Ravenous Baloth can be sacrificed for life before Death Cloud resolves, changing the safe value of X.

8 · Play patterns

How to pilot the deck

Count resources rather than permanents: a Wirewood Herald with Clamp may represent three cards, while an opposing expensive threat may represent only one.

Do not cast Death Cloud as a panic button unless the resulting topdeck game actually favors you.

Preserve a land, mana creature, Cemetery, Witness, or Solemn line that rebuilds immediately after the reset.

Against Affinity, stabilize first; a small Cloud that leaves Ravager or Nexus active may be worse than waiting for a complete answer.

9 · Post-board roles

Sideboarding principles

Affinity

Bring every Oxidize, Shaman, and Duplicant effect; trim slow recursion pieces that do not affect the first three turns.

Control

Persecute and recurring threats matter more than Echoing Decay. Build toward a medium Cloud that destroys the opponent’s hand and mana.

Goblins

Bottle Gnomes, Baloth, Decay, and Barter effects buy time. Avoid feeding Sharpshooter profitable chains.

Tooth and Nail

Creeping Mold and Persecute attack the mana and hand; a large Death Cloud can prevent the deck from ever reaching nine mana.
