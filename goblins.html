1 · Cover and deck identity

Red-Green Goblins

Goblins is a tribal aggro deck whose creatures form a resource engine. Warchief grants haste and reduces costs, Prospector creates mana, Sledder converts bodies into combat math, Sharpshooter turns deaths into repeated damage, and Siege-Gang converts a board into direct reach.

The Worlds green splash is a metagame adaptation rather than a philosophical change. Oxidize and Naturalize allow the deck to attack Affinity’s mana and equipment without abandoning the creature density required by Piledriver, Warchief, and Clickslither.

Critical-mass tribal

Many cards become powerful only when surrounded by other Goblins, rewarding high creature density.

Haste as combo

Warchief lets newly cast Piledrivers, Siege-Gangs, and utility creatures affect combat immediately.

Bodies are currency

Prospector, Sledder, Siege-Gang, Sharpshooter, and Clickslither all convert creatures into other resources.

Metagame splash

Green cards are chosen to answer artifacts while preserving the red tribal core.

2 · What this deck does

The strategic machine

Turns 1–2: establish bodies

Sledder or Prospector into Piledriver/Sparksmith creates either pressure or a control engine.

Turn 3: unlock haste

Goblin Warchief is the pivotal card because it changes the value and timing of every creature in hand.

Turns 3–5: build the lethal turn

Attack with protected Piledriver, sacrifice for Clickslither, or use Siege-Gang and Sharpshooter to bypass blockers.

Post-board: deny recovery

Stone Rain and Molten Rain punish Slide, Tooth, and U/W after they remove the first wave.

3 · History and evolution

How the archetype reached Worlds

Onslaught 2002

The tribal core is printed

Piledriver, Warchief, Sledder, Prospector, Sharpshooter, and Siege-Gang form one of the strongest linear creature packages in Standard history.

2003

Goblin Bidding becomes a premier deck

Black splashes and Patriarch’s Bidding give the tribe resilience and combo-like recovery.

Darksteel spring 2004

Skullclamp supercharges creature sacrifice

Clamp variants exploit expendable Goblins, but Affinity receives the same card in an even faster shell.

June 2004

The Clamp ban and Affinity pressure reshape the deck

The Worlds-era list becomes cleaner red-green aggro, with maindeck Oxidize and Electrostatic Bolt.

Worlds 2004

The archetype remains elite

Parker and Jensen go 5–1 with virtually identical 75s; Ryou Ogura reaches the semifinals overall.

October 2004

Onslaught rotation removes the tribe’s core

Piledriver, Warchief, Siege-Gang, and most of the engine leave Standard simultaneously.

5 · Mulligans and opening turns

Keep a plan, not a collection of powerful cards

Strong keeps

  • Two or three lands, a one-drop, and either Warchief or Piledriver.
  • Prospector plus Siege-Gang or multiple reduced-cost plays that create a turn-three/four burst.
  • Against Affinity, Oxidize/Electrostatic Bolt plus a competitive creature curve.
  • Against control after sideboarding, a fast opening plus Stone Rain or Molten Rain.

Common mulligans

  • Hands with only four- and five-mana creatures.
  • Green interaction without a green source.
  • Creature-light hands that make Piledriver, Warchief, and Sledder underperform.
  • Against Affinity, slow hands whose first meaningful card is Siege-Gang Commander.

ContextPreferred sequence
Default pressureOne-drop into Piledriver, then Warchief to create a hasty, protected attack.

Control post-boardThreat, land destruction, threat. Do not cast Stone Rain without pressure unless it prevents a sweeper immediately.
6 · Key interactions

Rules and sequencing that define the deck

Goblin Warchief reduces the generic portion of Goblin costs and grants haste to every Goblin, including itself.

Goblin Piledriver gains protection from blue and receives +2/+0 for each other attacking Goblin.

Skirk Prospector sacrifices a Goblin for red mana as a cost, allowing mana generation during combat or in response to removal.

Goblin Sharpshooter untaps whenever a creature dies; sacrifice outlets can create repeated damage chains.

Goblin Sledder can sacrifice a Goblin to alter combat after blockers, protecting a key creature or pushing damage.

Clickslither can sacrifice Goblins for +2/+2 and trample, converting a wide board into one large hasty threat.

Siege-Gang Commander provides four bodies, then converts Goblins into direct damage that bypasses combat.

7 · How to pilot the deck

Decision rules for real games

Treat Warchief as both a threat and a mana engine. Sometimes it should be held until it enables multiple hasty spells in one turn.

Count Piledriver damage before attacks and again after possible Sledder/Prospector sacrifices; protection from blue changes blocking options.

Do not sacrifice creatures casually. A body may be worth Piledriver damage, Sharpshooter untaps, Clickslither power, or Siege-Gang reach.

Sparksmith is a life-total resource. Use it to control the board, but do not make future activations impossible.

Against sweepers, preserve a Warchief or Siege-Gang in hand so the rebuilt board affects the game immediately.

Use artifact hate for tempo. Destroying Seat of the Synod may prevent Thoughtcast and Ravager in the same turn, which is better than killing a random creature.

Post-board land destruction requires a clock. Sequence it between creature waves rather than replacing the aggro plan.

8 · Sideboarding principles

Change roles without dismantling the engine

Naturalize supplements Oxidize against Plating, artifact lands, Astral Slide, and other noncreature permanents.

Stone Rain and Molten Rain transform the slow-control matchups by delaying Wrath, Vengeance, Tooth, and Cloudpost mana.

Gempalm Incinerator adds removal that cycles and scales with the tribe, improving creature mirrors without reducing Goblin count.

Do not bring every artifact card against decks with only incidental artifacts; diluted creature density weakens the entire engine.

Against Pyroclasm decks, emphasize Warchief timing, Clickslither, Siege-Gang, and controlled deployment.

10 · Historical notes and sources

What the archetype teaches

Goblins is a useful example of linear design with nonlinear play. The deck needs a high tribal count, yet almost every turn offers branching decisions because creatures can become mana, pump, removal, direct damage, or future Piledriver damage.
