Scope. This dossier follows Astral Slide from the Onslaught cycling-control family through the green-white Eternal Witness configuration that won Worlds. The focus is the post-Skullclamp, pre-Champions environment played in San Francisco.
1 · Cover and deck identity

Green-White Astral Slide

Astral Slide is not simply a blink deck. It is a control engine whose interaction is stored inside otherwise ordinary cycling cards. Once Slide is active, every cycled Renewed Faith or cycling land becomes a temporary removal spell, a protection spell, or a way to reuse Eternal Witness and Viridian Shaman.

The Worlds build was designed for a format in which Affinity forced opponents to devote enormous space to artifacts. Green converted that burden into card advantage: Viridian Shaman destroyed an artifact and then could be slid out and returned; Eternal Witness recycled Wrath, Oxidize, Plow Under, Decree, or another cycling card.

Engine over rate

Many cards are individually modest. Slide converts them into a repeatable interaction system.

Answers attached to bodies

Viridian Shaman and Eternal Witness remain useful after their first trigger because Slide can reset them.

Redundant late games

Eternal Dragon recursion, Decree tokens, Witness loops, and post-board Rude Awakening all close differently.

Metagame compression

Wrath and Akroma’s Vengeance answer creatures, artifact lands, equipment, and token armies without narrow one-for-one trades.

2 · What this deck does

The strategic machine

Turns 1–2: build colors

Cycle lands when necessary, Rampant Growth toward double white, and avoid spending cycling cards without a reason if Slide is likely to matter.

Turns 3–4: establish the engine

Resolve Astral Slide or deploy Shaman/Witness while preserving enough mana to cycle during combat.

Turns 4–7: reset the board

Wrath or Vengeance after extracting value from creatures. Use Slide to protect your own creatures or deny opposing modular, equipment, and combat damage.

Late game: recur inevitability

Eternal Dragon, Decree of Justice, repeated Witness triggers, and post-board Plow Under make ordinary topdecking unfavorable for the opponent.

3 · History and evolution

How the archetype reached Worlds

2002–2003

The cycling engine appears

Onslaught supplied Astral Slide, Lightning Rift, cycling lands, and a critical mass of cyclers. Early Standard versions were commonly red-white or three-color and used Slide as anti-creature control.

Early 2004

The format becomes artifact-centered

Mirrodin and Darksteel made Affinity the deck to beat. Slide already punished creature decks, but its removal suite had to expand toward artifacts and much faster openings.

June 2004

Skullclamp is banned

Affinity loses its most abusive draw engine, but Ravager, Disciple, artifact lands, and free creatures remain. Slide can now trade resources without being buried as quickly by Clamp.

Fifth Dawn summer

Green becomes the defining splash

Eternal Witness gives Slide a recursive engine, while Viridian Shaman and Oxidize turn the Affinity matchup into the deck’s central selling point.

Worlds 2004

Nuijten converts the metagame call

Julien Nuijten defeats Aeo Paquette’s Affinity in the final. Other successful pilots demonstrate Exalted Angel, Gilded Light, Duplicant, and Sundering Titan branches.

October 2004

Rotation ends this exact form

Champions of Kamigawa rotates Onslaught block, removing Slide, Eternal Dragon, Decree, and the cycling lands from Standard.

5 · Mulligans and opening turns

Keep a plan, not a collection of powerful cards

Strong keeps

  • Two or three lands with green and double-white access, plus Rampant Growth or early cycling.
  • Astral Slide with at least one cheap cycler and a realistic path to four mana.
  • Against Affinity, Viridian Shaman or Oxidize plus a sweeper and functional mana.
  • Against slow decks, Eternal Witness, cycling lands, and either Plow Under or a resilient finisher.

Common mulligans

  • Hands whose only early action is a seven-mana spell.
  • One-land hands that depend on cycling into both lands and colors.
  • Hands full of creature removal but no artifact interaction against known Affinity.
  • Multiple Eternal Dragons without enough early mana or cycling to survive.

ContextPreferred sequence
Unknown opponentPrioritize a green source, Rampant Growth, and a cycling land. Preserve Renewed Faith until its life or Slide trigger matters.
AffinityUse Oxidize/Shaman early enough to interrupt Plating or colored mana; do not greedily wait for maximum value.

U/W ControlCycle at end step, avoid exposing all threats to Wrath, and treat Decree and Dragon as the real game rather than Slide alone.
6 · Key interactions

Rules and sequencing that define the deck

Cycling creates a triggered ability from Astral Slide; countering or removing the cycling card does not undo a trigger already created.

A creature exiled by Slide returns at the beginning of the next end step. Sliding a creature during an end step normally keeps it away until the following end step.

Sliding Eternal Witness or Viridian Shaman resets it as a new object and produces another enters-the-battlefield trigger when it returns.

Slide can remove a creature carrying Cranial Plating or modular counters; attachments fall off and counters do not return with the new object.

Wrath of God can be cast after sliding your own Witness or Shaman, leaving the protected creature to return after the sweep.

Eternal Dragon’s plainscycling fixes early land drops, while its upkeep recursion supplies a repeatable late threat.

Decree of Justice can be cycled for tokens at instant speed; the triggered token production is not the same as casting the sorcery.

7 · How to pilot the deck

Decision rules for real games

Plan the entire turn around available cycling mana. Tapping out for a creature may sacrifice Slide’s combat control.

Identify whether Slide is a value engine or a survival tool. Against Affinity it often protects life and breaks equipment; against control it mostly reuses Witness.

Sequence Viridian Shaman targets to damage the opponent’s mana, not merely the most visible artifact. Artifact lands are often the highest-tempo target.

Do not cast Wrath merely to trade evenly if Slide can neutralize combat for another turn and create a larger sweep.

Use Witness proactively when mana would otherwise go unused; the body can later be recycled by Slide.

Count the opponent’s post-sweeper reach. Disciple drains, Shrapnel Blast, Blinkmoth Nexus, and Decree tokens all attack from different zones.

When ahead, shorten the game. Eternal Dragon recursion is inevitable, but giving Tooth or KCI unlimited draw steps is unnecessary.

8 · Sideboarding principles

Change roles without dismantling the engine

Against Affinity, increase cheap artifact interaction and preserve enough sweepers to answer the creature half of the deck.

Against Tooth and slow control, remove narrow creature answers for Plow Under, Creeping Mold, Rude Awakening, or other proactive cards.

Circle of Protection: Red is strongest when red damage is the opponent’s actual route to victory, not merely a few sideboard cards.

Scrabbling Claws checks Eternal Witness recursion, opposing Eternal Dragons, and some Ironworks lines while cycling when weak.

Gilded Light protects from Fireball, Mindslaver targeting, and lethal Disciple/Charbelcher-style effects, but does not answer a board of Myr tokens.

10 · Historical notes and sources

What the archetype teaches

Astral Slide won because it converted the format’s compulsory artifact answers into a coherent engine rather than a pile of hate cards. That is the deck’s main design lesson: narrow metagame cards become much stronger when the deck can reuse, cycle, or recover them.
