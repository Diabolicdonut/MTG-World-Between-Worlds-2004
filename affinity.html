Scope. This dossier covers post-Skullclamp Standard Affinity from Fifth Dawn through Worlds, including the Chrome Mox/Welding Jar and Aether Vial/Atog branches.
1 · Cover and deck identity

Ravager Affinity

Affinity’s strength came from stacking multiple discounts and conversion engines. Artifact lands paid for spells while increasing affinity counts; free creatures carried Cranial Plating; Ravager converted threatened artifacts into counters; Disciple converted the same sacrifices into direct damage.

The deck therefore changed roles without changing cards. It could flood the board, play a protected Plating threat, or stop attacking entirely and calculate a lethal Ravager–Disciple sacrifice chain. That role flexibility is why ordinary artifact removal rarely felt sufficient.

Mana and spell are the same object

Artifact lands cast spells, reduce affinity costs, feed Ravager, power Plating, and trigger Disciple when destroyed.

Redundant lethal axes

Combat, Plating, Disciple drains, Shrapnel Blast, Atog, Nexus, and Furnace Dragon demand different answers.

Virtual haste

Plating can move at instant speed and modular counters can move after combat, letting an unblocked creature become lethal.

Punishes narrow hate

One-for-one artifact removal can be sacrificed in response, while sweepers may still leave Disciple triggers or a large modular recipient.

2 · What this deck does

The strategic machine

Turn 1: establish artifact density

Artifact land plus Ornithopter, Worker, Sphere, Jar, Vial, or Chrome Mox sets up the discount engine.

Turn 2: create a must-answer permanent

Ravager, Disciple, or Cranial Plating forces the opponent to interact before their slower plan begins.

Turns 3–4: choose the kill

Attack with Plating, transfer modular counters, burn with Shrapnel Blast, or sacrifice the board through Disciple.

Post-board: transform around hate

Mana Leak, Annul, Pyroclasm, Oxidize, or Furnace Dragon let Affinity attack the opponent’s anti-Affinity plan rather than simply replay game one.

3 · History and evolution

How the archetype reached Worlds

October 2003

Mirrodin creates the shell

Artifact lands, affinity creatures, Thoughtcast, Disciple, and Atog establish an artifact aggro-combo strategy.

February 2004

Darksteel breaks the conversion engine

Arcbound Ravager and modular make every artifact disposable. Skullclamp makes the free-creature shell an overwhelming draw engine.

June 2004

Skullclamp is banned

The deck loses its easiest refuel mechanism, but the underlying mana and sacrifice engines remain intact.

Fifth Dawn

Cranial Plating restores explosiveness

Plating gives every small artifact creature immediate lethal potential, while cards such as Serum Visions and Engineered Explosives expand sideboard and splash options.

Worlds 2004

Multiple branches succeed

Paquette’s Chrome Mox/Jar build reaches the final; Häyrynen goes 6–0 with a conventional build; Cornelissen and Gulinski succeed with Vial and Atog.

March 2005

The environment finally removes the engine

Arcbound Ravager, Disciple of the Vault, and the six artifact lands are banned from Standard after months of continued dominance.

5 · Mulligans and opening turns

Keep a plan, not a collection of powerful cards

Strong keeps

  • Two artifact lands with a payoff such as Thoughtcast, Ravager, or Plating.
  • One land plus Chrome Mox or a dense zero/one-mana artifact sequence that casts Thoughtcast.
  • Ravager plus Disciple with enough artifacts to threaten a noncombat kill.
  • Against known artifact hate, a hand with Welding Jar, Thoughtcast, or a sideboard counter rather than only speed.

Common mulligans

  • Hands with expensive cards but insufficient artifact count.
  • No-colored-source hands that cannot cast Disciple, Thoughtcast, or Shrapnel Blast.
  • Multiple Cranial Platings without creatures.
  • Reactive post-board hands that have hate but no pressure.

Opening-turn priorities

ContextPreferred sequence
Aggressive defaultArtifact land, zero/one-mana artifacts, then Ravager or Plating. Count turn-three damage before choosing the second-turn threat.
Against Wrath decksLead with threats that preserve value—Nexus, Ravager, Jar—and avoid committing every free body without Disciple or Thoughtcast.
Against red sweepersPrioritize Ravager and larger affinity creatures; keep Worker or Nexus available as modular recipients.
Post-board combo planUse Annul/Mana Leak to protect the decisive permanent rather than fighting every piece of hate.
6 · Key interactions

Rules and sequencing that define the deck

Ravager can sacrifice any artifact, including artifact lands, Welding Jar, Cranial Plating, and itself.

When Ravager dies, modular targets an artifact creature; the recipient must still be legal when the trigger resolves.

Disciple triggers for each artifact put into your graveyard from the battlefield, including artifacts sacrificed in response to removal.

Cranial Plating’s black-black equip ability may be activated at instant speed, including after blockers are declared.

Blinkmoth Nexus becomes an artifact creature, can receive modular counters, and adds to Plating while animated.

Atog and Ravager can consume the same category of resources but require different damage calculations; do not sacrifice away a safer lethal line.

Furnace Dragon’s affinity for artifacts can reduce its cost dramatically, and its trigger can exile the opponent’s artifact board while your Dragon remains.

7 · How to pilot the deck

Decision rules for real games

Before every attack, count three versions of lethal: current combat, Plating movement, and full sacrifice through Ravager/Disciple.

Do not sacrifice artifacts merely because you can. Artifact count is mana, Thoughtcast cost, Plating power, and future Disciple damage.

Sequence artifact lands last when ordinary lands or zero-mana artifacts can cast the same spell; this reduces exposure to Shatter and Viridian Shaman.

Against sweepers, decide whether Ravager should consolidate counters or preserve multiple post-Wrath bodies through modular.

Use Thoughtcast before committing to an all-in line unless the extra mana or artifact must remain available for protection.

When the opponent has instant-speed artifact removal, make them act before moving Plating or sacrificing the board.

Post-board, recognize when you are the control deck in the mirror. Pyroclasm, Electrostatic Bolt, and Oxidize may matter more than the fastest draw.

8 · Sideboarding principles

Change roles without dismantling the engine

Choose the transformation that attacks the opponent’s specific hate: counters for Vengeance/Slide, Pyroclasm for creature mirrors, Furnace Dragon for artifact mirrors.

Do not overload on colored sideboard cards without preserving enough Glimmervoids, colored artifact lands, Spheres, or Chrome Moxen.

Welding Jar protects against destroy effects but not Oxidize? It does protect from destroy, while exile, sacrifice, and toughness reduction require different responses.

Against Wrath decks, trim the weakest ground creatures before trimming Thoughtcast or the engines that rebuild.

In the mirror, Disciple, Ravager, and artifact-land timing are more important than raw creature count.

10 · Historical notes and sources

What the archetype teaches

Affinity’s lesson is not merely that artifact lands were too strong. The deck’s real power was systemic: every permanent participated in several engines simultaneously, so destroying one object rarely removed all of its value.
