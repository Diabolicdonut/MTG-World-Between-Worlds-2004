1 · Deck identity

U/W Proteus Control

Proteus Control begins as conventional U/W control—Mana Leak, Condescend, Wrath of God, Pulse of the Fields, Thirst for Knowledge, and Decree of Justice. Its unusual deck-building constraint is that Darksteel Colossus is the only creature card. Proteus Staff can therefore turn a Raise the Alarm or Decree token into an eleven-mana indestructible threat without ever drawing or casting it normally.

2 · Strategic role

What this deck does

The deck controls the first several turns, produces a disposable token, and activates Proteus Staff while protecting the sequence with countermagic. Because the deck contains only one creature, the Staff reveal is deterministic whenever Colossus remains in the library. The same token cards also function normally as blockers and win conditions, so the combo pieces are less dead than a traditional two-card combo.

History and evolution

U/W control gains breathing room

A slower field makes a three-mana artifact and three-mana activation more realistic.

Jason Howden reaches the Top 8

The one-creature construction demonstrates a deterministic Staff package inside a proven U/W control frame.

Road to Worlds

A respected rogue branch, not a pillar

The deck remains vulnerable to artifact hate and Affinity speed, so it influences preparation without becoming a dominant Worlds archetype.

4 · Construction logic

Key design characteristics

The one-creature rule

Every additional creature would make Proteus Staff less deterministic. Token-producing spells provide bodies without changing the library reveal.

Two plans in one shell

Raise the Alarm and Decree are defensive cards and ordinary win conditions even when Staff is removed.

Control infrastructure

Wrath, Pulse, Renewed Faith, counters, and Thirst let the deck play a long game without assembling the combo quickly.

Artifact vulnerability

Staff gives opposing Shatter, Oxidize, Annul, and Viridian Shaman a high-value target in a format already full of artifact hate.

6 · Opening decisions

Mulligans and opening turns

Keep

  • Three lands with blue and white, plus an early counter or Raise the Alarm.
  • A Staff hand that also contains token production and enough mana to activate without skipping defense.
  • Thirst for Knowledge with an excess artifact or redundant high-cost card to discard.

Send back

  • Hands containing Darksteel Colossus without a useful way to recycle or win a normal control game.
  • Staff plus no token maker and no card selection.
  • Temple-heavy hands that cannot cast interaction before the fifth land arrives.

Opening priorities

  1. Represent Mana Leak on turn two rather than tapping out for Staff into an obvious must-answer threat.
  2. Create the token at the opponent’s end step whenever possible, shortening the window for sorcery-speed removal.
  3. Against artifact decks, decide whether Staff is realistically going to survive; a conventional Decree control plan may be stronger.
7 · Rules and sequencing

Key interactions

Proteus Staff puts the targeted creature on the bottom of its owner’s library, then reveals cards until a creature is found. With only Darksteel Colossus in the deck, the result is deterministic.

If Darksteel Colossus would be put into a graveyard, its replacement effect shuffles it into the library, restoring the Staff target.

Raise the Alarm creates creatures without adding creature cards to the library, preserving the one-creature constraint.

Staff can target an opposing creature, but that player also reveals until a replacement creature is found; use this only when the likely result is favorable.

8 · Play patterns

How to pilot the deck

Play as U/W control first. The Staff line is a finisher, not permission to ignore the opponent’s board.

Protect the activation rather than merely the artifact; instant-speed removal on the target token can make the ability fail.

Use Thirst to discard extra Staffs or an awkwardly drawn Colossus only when its shuffle replacement or future plan is understood.

Know when Decree tokens are already enough. Exposing Staff may be unnecessary against a control opponent with artifact answers stranded in hand.

9 · Post-board roles

Sideboarding principles

Affinity

Annul, Stifle, and Acquire are the most important cards. The Staff plan is secondary to surviving Ravager and Plating.

Tooth and Nail

Stifle and counters fight Mindslaver and Tooth; Acquire can steal Colossus or a key artifact threat.

Goblins

Preserve Wrath, Pulse, and token blockers. Staff is slow unless the board is already contained.

U/W and Slide

Scrabbling Claws limits Eternal Dragon and Witness recursion; protect Staff only when it represents a faster clock than Decree.
