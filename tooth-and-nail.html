1 · Cover and deck identity

Tooth and Nail

Tooth and Nail is a big-mana deck with a combo finish, but its real strength is threat density. Sylvan Scrying and Reap and Sow build Tron; Solemn Simulacrum and Eternal Witness generate value; Oblivion Stone and Pyroclasm reset the board; Mindslaver and Tooth demand hard answers.

Entwined Tooth is the headline, yet the deck wins many games without resolving it. Tron allows hard-cast Sundering Titan, Darksteel Colossus, repeated Mindslaver activations, and oversized Oblivion Stone turns.

Land tutors are engine pieces

Scrying and Reap and Sow are not simple ramp; they assemble a seven-mana three-land engine.

Threats overlap with answers

Triskelion/Vampire clears creatures, Sundering Titan attacks mana, and Duplicant answers a resolved monster.

Inevitability through density

Control decks may counter one Tooth, but Witness, Mindslaver, and another topdeck keep presenting must-answer cards.

Flexible survival packages

Red versions use Pyroclasm; mono-green versions use Vine Trellis, Oxidize, and more bodies.

2 · What this deck does

The strategic machine

Turns 1–3: assemble mana

Cast Scrying, Trellis, Talisman, Solemn, or Reap and Sow to secure green and Tron pieces.

Turns 3–5: survive

Pyroclasm, Oxidize, and Oblivion Stone prevent Affinity or Goblins from ending the game before Tron matters.

Turns 4–6: present a must-answer

Mindslaver, Sundering Titan, or a hard-cast creature forces the opponent to spend counters before Tooth.

Endgame: select the correct pair

Choose Triskelion/Vampire for creatures, Titan plus pressure for mana, or Colossus plus a complementary threat when raw power is enough.

3 · History and evolution

How the archetype reached Worlds

Eighth Edition 2003

Urzatron returns to Standard

Mine, Power Plant, and Tower provide the mana base needed to cast Mirrodin’s expensive artifacts and Tooth and Nail.

Mirrodin

The namesake and control shell arrive

Tooth and Nail, Mindslaver, Oblivion Stone, Solemn Simulacrum, Triskelion, and artifact threats form the first complete engine.

Darksteel

The top end becomes more punishing

Darksteel Colossus and Sundering Titan create devastating Tooth packages and hard-cast threats.

Fifth Dawn

Witness and Vampire improve flexibility

Eternal Witness strengthens recursion, while Mephidross Vampire creates a board-clearing combo with Triskelion.

Worlds 2004

Two branches post elite records

Sasha Zorc goes 6–0 with a red Pyroclasm splash; Jan Škampa goes 5–1 with Vine Trellis and maindeck Oxidize.

Post-rotation

Kamigawa refines rather than replaces it

Sakura-Tribe Elder, Kodama’s Reach, Boseiju, Kiki-Jiki, and Sensei’s Divining Top create a new generation after Onslaught rotates.

5 · Mulligans and opening turns

Keep a plan, not a collection of powerful cards

Strong keeps

  • Two or three lands with green mana plus Sylvan Scrying, Trellis, Talisman, or Solemn.
  • Two different Tron pieces plus a tutor and an early defensive spell.
  • Against Affinity, Pyroclasm/Oxidize or Oblivion Stone with a credible path to cast it.
  • Against control, mana development plus multiple must-counter spells rather than a single Tooth.

Common mulligans

  • Hands containing only colorless Tron pieces and no green source.
  • Large-creature hands with no ramp or tutor.
  • Slow Tron hands with no interaction against known Affinity or Goblins.
  • Hands that depend on drawing the exact third Tron piece naturally while doing nothing else.

Opening-turn priorities

ContextPreferred sequence
Default Tron lineGreen source into Sylvan Scrying, then choose the missing Tron piece that preserves future green.
Defensive lineTrellis/Talisman or Pyroclasm first when the opponent can deal lethal before Tron assembles.
Oblivion Stone lineDevelop Stone before completing Tron if a reset is required; activate only after extracting value from Solemn or fate counters.
Against countersLead with Mindslaver, Reap and Sow, or a hard-cast threat to tax permission before Tooth.
6 · Key interactions

Rules and sequencing that define the deck

The three Urza lands produce seven total mana only when all three differently named pieces are controlled.

Entwined Tooth searches for up to two creatures and puts up to two creatures from hand into play; the two functions can use different cards.

Triskelion plus Mephidross Vampire allows Triskelion to receive a +1/+1 counter whenever a creature it damaged dies, potentially clearing an opposing board.

Reap and Sow may tutor a land or destroy one; entwine does both, which is especially powerful against opposing Tron or Cloudpost.

Oblivion Stone destroys nonland permanents without fate counters. Its activation is separate from casting it and can be used later.

Mindslaver controls the opponent’s next turn, including choices, mana use, attacks, and spell targets within the rules.

Eternal Witness returns Tooth, Stone, Mindslaver, or land tutors, making discard and counters less final than they appear.

7 · How to pilot the deck

Decision rules for real games

Green mana is more important than raw Tron count in the opening hand. A seven-mana engine that cannot cast Scrying is not functional.

Choose Tooth targets based on the board, not habit. Triskelion/Vampire is poor into an empty control board; Sundering Titan may be game-ending there.

Use Reap and Sow as land destruction when the tempo swing exceeds the value of another Tron piece.

Do not activate Oblivion Stone automatically. Sometimes its presence freezes the opponent and buys more time than the sweep itself.

Sequence Solemn Simulacrum so it fixes green before Tron is assembled and becomes expendable after the deck reaches seven or nine mana.

Against counters, present multiple different threats. The deck wins by exhausting relevant answers, not by protecting one sacred Tooth.

Track library and graveyard creature packages. Drawing one half of a combo changes what Tooth can search and what it can put from hand into play.

8 · Sideboarding principles

Change roles without dismantling the engine

Oxidize, Viridian Shaman, and Pyroclasm are the cards that let the big-mana engine survive Affinity’s early turns.

Dwarven Blastminer and additional Sundering Titans attack slow multicolor and Cloudpost mana bases.

Relic Barrier can buy time against Plating, artifact lands, and KCI mana without committing to a one-shot destruction spell.

Creeping Mold and additional Reap and Sow strengthen the mirror and punish Slide/U/W mana engines.

Do not sideboard out so much ramp that the deck becomes a pile of expensive answers and creatures.

10 · Historical notes and sources

What the archetype teaches

The archetype illustrates the difference between a combo card and a combo deck. Tooth and Nail is the spectacular finish, but the deck’s competitive structure is the mana engine, threat density, and ability to make every late topdeck consequential.
