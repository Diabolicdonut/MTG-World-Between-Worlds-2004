1 · Cover and deck identity

Blue-Red March–Obliterate

U/R March is a control deck whose lock is embedded in a rules interaction. March of the Machines animates artifacts; Darksteel Ingot becomes an indestructible 3/3; Obliterate destroys lands, creatures, and ordinary artifacts but cannot destroy the Ingot. The opponent is reset to nothing while the Ingot attacks.

March is also maindeck Affinity hate. Artifact lands have mana value zero, so March turns them into 0/0 artifact creatures that are put into the graveyard. This gives the namesake enchantment both a fair control function and a combo finish.

A win condition that is also hate

March attacks artifact lands and mana while enabling the post-Obliterate kill.

Indestructible infrastructure

Darksteel Ingot fixes mana, survives Obliterate, and becomes the creature that wins.

Broad cheap interaction

Burn, Pyroclasm, Echoing Truth, Annul, Mana Leak, and Condescend cover different engines.

Slow inevitability from unusual cards

Future Sight or Concentrate supplies the cards needed to assemble a seven/eight-mana reset safely.

2 · What this deck does

The strategic machine

Turns 1–3: answer tempo

Burn creatures, Pyroclasm wide boards, Annul artifact engines, and use Serum Visions to hit land drops.

Turns 3–5: establish indestructible mana

Resolve Darksteel Ingot while preserving enough interaction to avoid losing initiative.

Turns 4–7: deploy March selectively

Against Affinity, March may immediately destroy artifact lands. Against control, it may be held until Obliterate is ready.

Endgame: reset asymmetrically

Resolve Obliterate with March and Ingot in place, then attack with the surviving 3/3 artifacts.

3 · History and evolution

How the archetype reached Worlds

Mirrodin 2003

March of the Machines enters an artifact block

The enchantment is initially a build-around and a peculiar answer to artifact lands.

Darksteel 2004

Indestructible artifacts complete the lock

Darksteel Ingot can survive mass artifact destruction and become a creature under March.

Eighth Edition interaction

Obliterate supplies the asymmetric reset

Because Obliterate does not destroy enchantments and cannot destroy indestructible Ingot, the board rebuild is one-sided.

Fifth Dawn summer

Selection and counters improve

Serum Visions and Condescend increase consistency in a deck that must hit lands, interaction, and a multi-card engine.

Worlds 2004

Three pilots post 5–1 records

Marco Blume, Lars Dam, and Craig Krempels register distinct but clearly related configurations.

After rotation

The metagame moves away

The core cards remain for a time, but the removal of Onslaught threats and arrival of Kamigawa produces different blue-red control priorities.

5 · Mulligans and opening turns

Keep a plan, not a collection of powerful cards

Strong keeps

  • Three lands with both colors, early interaction, and either Ingot or card selection.
  • Two lands plus Serum Visions and cheap counters/burn when the curve is functional.
  • Against Affinity, Pyroclasm, Electrostatic Bolt, Annul, or March with enough mana to cast them.
  • Against control, Ingot plus card draw and a permission spell.

Common mulligans

  • Hands containing March and Obliterate but no early defense.
  • Two-Mountain or two-Island hands that cannot cast the other half of the deck.
  • Expensive draw spells without enough lands or Serum Visions.
  • Against Affinity, hands whose first play is Darksteel Ingot.

Opening-turn priorities

ContextPreferred sequence
AffinityUse Bolt/Pyroclasm/Annul first. March on turn four may erase artifact lands, but only if life total survives.
ControlDevelop Ingot and card draw; avoid exposing March to enchantment removal long before Obliterate.
Tooth and NailCounter Scrying/Reap when it constrains Tron, then use Obliterate before Mindslaver or Tooth becomes decisive.
UnknownSerum Visions prioritizes the fourth land and a mix of interaction plus engine, not simply the most powerful card.
6 · Key interactions

Rules and sequencing that define the deck

March of the Machines makes each noncreature artifact a creature with power and toughness equal to mana value.

Artifact lands have mana value zero, so under March they become 0/0 creatures and are put into the graveyard.

Darksteel Ingot becomes a 3/3 indestructible artifact creature and can still tap for mana if it is untapped.

Obliterate cannot be countered and destroys artifacts, creatures, and lands; it does not destroy enchantments, and indestructible Ingot survives.

If March leaves the battlefield after Obliterate, surviving Ingots stop being creatures but remain mana sources for rebuilding.

Echoing Truth can return multiple permanents with the same name, including token swarms or repeated artifact lands after animation.

Future Sight allows lands and spells to be played from the top, but information and sequencing become public and must be managed carefully.

7 · How to pilot the deck

Decision rules for real games

Do not treat March as a card that must wait for Obliterate. Against Affinity, destroying several artifact lands may be the entire game.

Conversely, do not cast March into a board where animating opposing artifacts creates lethal attackers without an immediate sweep.

Darksteel Ingot is the deck’s most important permanent. It fixes colors, accelerates, survives the reset, and becomes the clock.

Use counters on engine cards—Tooth, Ironworks, Slide, Plating—not on every creature that burn can answer.

Plan the Obliterate turn several turns ahead: life total, cards in hand, surviving Ingots, and the opponent’s non-destroyed enchantments all matter.

After Obliterate, attack while rebuilding mana. The lock is not infinite; a slow clock gives the opponent time to draw lands and answers.

Future Sight versions should clear unwanted top cards with Serum Visions or other draw before passing priority unnecessarily.

8 · Sideboarding principles

Change roles without dismantling the engine

Arc-Slogger transforms the deck into a more conventional control strategy when opponents overload on enchantment or combo hate.

Shatter and additional Annul increase artifact interaction without depending entirely on March.

Last Word improves control and combo fights where resolving March, Future Sight, or a counter war is decisive.

Bribery is a high-impact answer to Tooth and other large-creature decks, but should not replace the core mana plan.

Dwarven Blastminer and Millstone in the Dam list attack slow mana and control mirrors from different angles.

10 · Historical notes and sources

What the archetype teaches

U/R March is the most visibly “designed” deck in the Worlds field: its mana rock, hate card, lock piece, and win condition are different roles of the same permanents. The deck rewards understanding state-based actions and asymmetric resets.
