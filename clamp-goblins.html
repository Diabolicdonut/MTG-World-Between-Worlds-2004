Scope of this guide

Year position: This dossier follows Goblins across the Skullclamp and post-ban environments. The Worlds R/G Goblins page remains the dedicated September list guide.
1 · Deck identity

Skullclamp Goblins and Goblin Bidding

Goblins is not merely a curve of red creatures. Warchief compresses mana and grants haste, Prospector converts bodies into mana, Sharpshooter turns deaths into repeated damage, and Bidding rebuilds the entire battlefield. Skullclamp briefly made every expendable Goblin draw two cards.

2 · Game plan

What this deck does

Develop a critical mass of Goblins rather than trading one-for-one.

Use Warchief to convert the next turn into a hasty burst rather than a normal creature turn.

Exploit Prospector, Sledder, Sharpshooter, and Siege-Gang as a network of sacrifice and death triggers.

After the ban, use Patriarch’s Bidding as the card-advantage and combo reset.

3 · Development

History and evolution

Start of 2004

Goblin Bidding inherited

Onslaught block already supplied the tribal core and a graveyard-combo endgame.

Darksteel

Skullclamp joins the tribe

One-toughness bodies become cards, and Prospector makes drawing through the deck mana-neutral in explosive turns.

Grand Prix Brussels

Goblins fills six Top 8 seats alongside Affinity

The deck is the clearest nonartifact beneficiary of Skullclamp.

June 20

The ban removes Clamp

Lists return to Piledriver, Gempalm, Bidding, or green artifact hate.

Worlds

R/G anti-Affinity Goblins

Oxidize replaces the banned draw engine, while Warchief and Siege-Gang remain the clock.

4 · Construction

Key design characteristics

Tribal cost compression

Warchief makes multiple-spell turns possible and turns topdecked Goblins into immediate damage.

Sacrifice economy

Prospector and Sledder make removal and combat less clean for the opponent.

Board-dependent power

The deck is devastating with several Goblins and mediocre when forced into isolated topdecks.

Sideboard transformation

Land destruction attacks Tooth and control; artifact removal attacks Affinity; Bidding attacks attrition.

6 · Opening decisions

Mulligans and opening turns

Keep

  • At least two lands and a one- or two-mana Goblin.
  • Warchief plus enough creatures to exploit the discount.
  • Against Affinity, hands containing Electrostatic Bolt, Shatter, Oxidize, or Sharpshooter.

Send back

  • Hands that begin at three mana without Prospector.
  • Multiple Siege-Gangs or Clickslithers with no early development.
  • Bidding hands with no creatures likely to reach the graveyard.

Opening priorities

  1. Lead with Prospector when acceleration matters; lead with Sledder when protecting a board matters more.
  2. Decide whether Warchief is a setup card or should be held for a same-turn haste burst.
  3. Against sweepers, make the opponent act before deploying the second wave.
7 · Rules and sequencing

Key interactions

Goblin Warchief reduces Goblin costs and gives Goblins haste, including creatures returned by Patriarch’s Bidding.

Goblin Sharpshooter untaps whenever any creature dies, enabling chains with Prospector and combat.

Skirk Prospector can sacrifice Goblins for mana in response to removal or after combat damage was stacked under period rules.

Patriarch’s Bidding returns the chosen creature type for every player; verify the opponent’s graveyard before naming Goblin.

8 · Play patterns

How to pilot the deck

  1. Count Piledriver damage before combat and again after each possible blocker or sacrifice.
  2. Treat Siege-Gang as both a board and a bank of future damage; do not throw tokens away without a reason.
  3. Against Affinity, kill Ravager or Disciple at the point that constrains their sacrifice tree, not merely the first legal moment.
  4. With Bidding, trade creatures aggressively if the graveyard reset favors you.
9 · Post-board roles

Sideboarding principles

Against Affinity

Bring artifact removal and cheap burn; trim expensive grind cards that do not affect the first three turns.

Against control

Bring land destruction, Sulfuric Vortex, or Bidding; lower exposure to Wrath by preserving haste threats.

Against creature mirrors

Sharpshooter, Sparksmith, Starstorm, and Gempalm determine who can keep a board.

After the Clamp ban

Replace lost card flow with higher creature density, Bidding, or direct metagame interaction.
